How other states will be added

EPA does not test private wells. It points well owners to each state’s drinking-water laboratory certification program. The August 2025 EPA compilation of those programs is linked from EPA’s certification contact page (page last updated 11 August 2026):

EPA: Contact information for certification programs and certified laboratories

We will not publish a state page until we can transcribe a current official list (or honestly say what we could and could not verify). See methodology and README for the nightly-page workflow.

National context

The U.S. Geological Survey states that more than 43 million people — about 15 percent of the U.S. population — rely on domestic (private) wells, and that the quality and safety of that water are not regulated by the federal Safe Drinking Water Act or, in most cases, by state laws. Homeowners are responsible for testing. Source: USGS Domestic (Private) Supply Wells (page retrieved 29 August 2026).

Methodology and disclosure · State directory

This site is not a government agency and does not certify laboratories. Listings are transcribed from official state and federal sources cited on each page. Accreditation, phone numbers, and sample-acceptance policies change. Confirm with the laboratory and the state certification program before you ship a sample.

Private wells are not regulated under the federal Safe Drinking Water Act in the way public water systems are. You are responsible for testing.
