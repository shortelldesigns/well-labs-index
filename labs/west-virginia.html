West Virginia labs

Two paths are independently verified for private well owners through the West Virginia Department of Health and Human Resources (DHHR) Office of Laboratory Services (OLS): (1) Environmental Microbiology tests water from private, individual households (coliform / E. coli); OLS says private citizens should first contact their local county health department so a sanitarian can collect the sample, and samples must use OLS sterile bottles; (2) Environmental Chemistry states it provides support to private well owners and lists an initial screening set of total coliform, regulated metals, and combined nitrate + nitrite — order bottles by phone or form before you collect. Municipal / utility water-plant labs, the state agriculture Moorefield field office, OLS state labs, and all out-of-state labs are omitted from the commercial table. Call before you collect water.

Verified for private well owners

OLS Environmental Microbiology (private / individual households)

OLS Environmental Microbiology · Microbiology bottle request form (PDF) · Local health departments map

OLS states the Environmental Microbiology Unit tests water from the majority of public water systems and also tests water from private, individual households, recreational areas, bottled water companies, and dairy plants/farms. Tests begin with coliform bacteria; positives are followed for fecal coliforms / E. coli.

OLS also states: on a limited basis and by prior arrangement, samples are received from private citizens. Private citizens needing to have their well water tested should first contact their local county health departments so a trained sanitarian can collect the sample and ensure the well is properly protected.

Samples must be collected in OLS-provided sterile containers (dechlorinating agent; sterility-tested), supplied after the Bottle Request Form. Samples must be received and testing begun within 30 hours of collection. Containers are state property and must be returned to OLS — they cannot be sent to another laboratory.

Receipt hours (as printed): South Charleston Laboratory — 8:00 a.m. to 5:00 p.m. Monday through Friday (except state holidays), U.S. Mail, and by special arrangements on Saturday. Bardane District Environmental Laboratory — 8:00 a.m. to 4:00 p.m. Monday through Wednesday and 8:00 a.m. to 12:00 p.m. on Thursday (Eastern panhandle counties: Berkeley, Jefferson, Grant, Hampshire, Hardy, Mineral, Morgan, Pendleton, and Tucker).

Contacts (as printed on the OLS page): Water Testing 304-414-2432 · Laboratory Certification 304-414-2435 · Kearneysville (Bardane) 304-725-5832 · OLS main office 167 11th Ave, South Charleston, WV 25303 · 304-558-3530. Certified-list addresses: OLS Microbiology, 4710 Chimney Drive, Suite G, Charleston, WV 25302 (Certificate 00003 M) · District Environmental Laboratory, 1948 Wiltshire Road, Suite #7, Kearneysville, WV 25430 (Certificate 00005 M).

Fees: Confirm current amounts with OLS — this directory does not republish a fee table.

OLS Environmental Chemistry (private well owners)

OLS Environmental Chemistry · Bottle request form (Apr 2026 PDF) · Information for Private Well Owners (PDF)

OLS states the Environmental Chemistry Section provides support to the Office of Environmental Health Services, County Health Departments, the Oral Health Program, and private well owners. An initial set of tests private well owners can use includes total coliform, regulated metals, and combined nitrate + nitrite.

How to order sample bottles (as printed): telephone 304-965-2694, or complete the bottle request form. Please allow about one week for delivery (per the form).

Contact: Environmental Chemistry Section, 4710 Chimney Drive, Suite G, Charleston, WV 25302 · Phone 304-965-2694 · Fax 304-965-2696. Certified-list entry: Certificate 00003 C (Inorganics; Trace Metals).

Methodology and disclosure · State directory

This site is not a government agency and does not certify laboratories. Listings are transcribed from official state and federal sources cited on each page. Accreditation, phone numbers, and sample-acceptance policies change. Confirm with the laboratory and the state certification program before you ship a sample.

Private wells are not regulated under the federal Safe Drinking Water Act in the way public water systems are. You are responsible for testing.
