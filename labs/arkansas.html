Verified for private well owners

ADH Glen F. Baker — Private/Well Water Testing (kits via local health unit)

Water Microbiology Laboratory · Find a local health unit · Local Health Unit Directory PDF

Ensuring the safety of well water is the responsibility of each individual. Whether testing for personal use or as a mortgage requirement, start by obtaining a Water Collection Kit from your local health unit. Private drinking water samples can only be tested for total coliform bacteria and E. coli and must be received within 30 hours of collection. The lab does not evaluate ponds, creeks, or other non-drinking sources, and does not test for minerals, parasites, or chemicals on this path.

Contacts (as printed on the Water Microbiology / Contact pages): Glen F. Baker Public Health Laboratory, 201 S. Monroe St., Little Rock, AR 72205 · email adh.lab@arkansas.gov · ADH 4815 W. Markham, Little Rock, AR 72205-3867 · 1-800-462-0599.

Fees: Confirm current amounts with ADH / your local health unit — this directory does not republish the instructions-PDF fee line.

Sample collection instructions + return / hand-delivery

ADH Private Drinking Water Sample Collection Instructions (PDF) · alternate host: Instructions_for_Private_Water_Collection.pdf

Follow the official collection steps (do not rinse bottles; indoor faucet preferred; flame and flush as directed; fill to the 100 mL line). Complete the sample form; enclose payment as the instructions require. Within 30 hours, return the sample to your Local County Health unit before the ADH courier picks up for the day, or hand deliver to the Glen F. Baker Public Health Laboratory at 201 South Monroe Street, Little Rock, AR 72205. Samples are received in the laboratory between 8:00 a.m. and 4:00 p.m., Monday through Friday (except legal holidays). Samples will not be accepted at the Local Health Unit after the courier pick-up time on Thursday and before the first working day of the following week.

Methodology and disclosure · State directory

This site is not a government agency and does not certify laboratories. Listings are transcribed from official state and federal sources cited on each page. Accreditation, phone numbers, and sample-acceptance policies change. Confirm with the laboratory and the state certification program before you ship a sample.

Private wells are not regulated under the federal Safe Drinking Water Act in the way public water systems are. You are responsible for testing.
