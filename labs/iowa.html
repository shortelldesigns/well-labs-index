Iowa labs

Three State Hygienic Laboratory locations are independently verified for private-well testing (Coralville, Lakeside, Ankeny). Iowa DNR’s Private Well Testing page also points well owners to county environmental health offices and the Private Well Grants Program for free testing of recommended analytes. The commercial table lists 11 in-state Commercial laboratories whose LabCert Program includes Drinking Water. LabCert does not say which commercial labs accept homeowner samples — call before you collect water.

Sources retrieved 7 September 2026. Official roster: Iowa DNR LabCert — List of Certified Labs (Date Generated: 9/7/2026). Private-well guidance: DNR Well Testing · Private Well Program. SHL homeowner kit: Request Private Well Testing.

What we could and could not verify

  • LabCert generated 9/7/2026 was archived under data/sources/ia-labcert-lablisting-2026-09-07.html. Only rows with Program including Drinking Water are considered for this page.
  • In-state Commercial Drinking Water labs: 11 tabulated. Out-of-state Commercial Drinking Water labs on the same roster are omitted — use the official LabCert listing.
  • Municipal utility / water works / WWTP / WRF / regional water association labs are omitted even when they hold Drinking Water certification. Industrial/Private labs (including Iowa Soybean Association) and Nonpotable-only rows are omitted. “DNR (Test - Not a Lab)” is omitted.
  • LabCert does not label private-well acceptance for commercial labs. Every commercial row is “unknown — call first.” Mail vs. drop-off is unknown.
  • SHL is verified because DNR Well Testing and SHL’s request form both support private-well testing (coliform bacteria and nitrate kit). County health labs with Drinking Water are listed separately; acceptance at each address is unknown unless you call — DNR says counties can arrange testing via Private Well Grants.
  • Analysis prices are intentionally omitted. DNR’s page lists Private Well Grants service funding amounts for shock chlorination, assessment, plugging, and reconstruction; those grant figures are not copied onto this page.

Verified for private well owners

State Hygienic Laboratory at the University of Iowa — three LabCert locations with Drinking Water. Start with the Request Private Well Testing form, or contact your county environmental health office about Private Well Grants (PWG) free testing for recommended analytes (DNR recommends at least annual bacteria/coliform and nitrate, plus arsenic and manganese on a multi-year schedule — see the official page).

Methodology and disclosure · State directory

This site is not a government agency and does not certify laboratories. Listings are transcribed from official state and federal sources cited on each page. Accreditation, phone numbers, and sample-acceptance policies change. Confirm with the laboratory and the state certification program before you ship a sample.

Private wells are not regulated under the federal Safe Drinking Water Act in the way public water systems are. You are responsible for testing.
