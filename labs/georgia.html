Georgia labs

Two paths are independently verified for private wells: (1) UGA Agricultural and Environmental Services Laboratories (AESL) private-well chemical testing — including the DPH-recommended W33C Private Well Chemical Test — submitted through your county UGA Cooperative Extension office; (2) annual bacterial testing that DPH says may be available through your local County Environmental Health office. The table below lists 14 in-state laboratories from Georgia EPD’s January 9, 2025 commercial microbiology and chemical certification PDFs. Commercial private-well acceptance is unknown except for UGA AESL — call before you collect water.

Sources retrieved 12 September 2026. Private-well guidance: DPH Well Water · DPH well testing fact sheet · County Environmental Health directory · UGA AESL water analyses · Find your Extension office. Certified lab hub: EPD Drinking Water (chemical and microbiology PDFs revised January 9, 2025).

Verified for private well owners

UGA AESL — Private Well Chemical Test (W33C) via Extension

Start with your local UGA Cooperative Extension office or AESL water pages: AESL Water Analyses · Feed and Environmental Water Laboratory: (706) 542-5350 · 2300 College Station Road, Athens, GA 30602

DPH (retrieved 12 September 2026) recommends a bacterial test annually and a chemical screening about every three years. The W33C Private Well Chemical Test (includes arsenic and lead) is available through county Extension offices. AESL also publishes other homeowner water forms (including microbiological tests and home-loan closing profiles) — get bottles and instructions from Extension before you collect. Confirm current fees and turnaround with your county office. Prices intentionally omitted from this directory.

Source: DPH Well Water · DPH fact sheet · AESL water · Extension offices.

County Environmental Health — bacterial testing when offered

DPH states annual total coliform / fecal coliform testing may be available through your local County Environmental Health office. Services and fees vary by county — call first. Directory: DPH Environmental Health contacts.

Methodology and disclosure · State directory

This site is not a government agency and does not certify laboratories. Listings are transcribed from official state and federal sources cited on each page. Accreditation, phone numbers, and sample-acceptance policies change. Confirm with the laboratory and the state certification program before you ship a sample.

Private wells are not regulated under the federal Safe Drinking Water Act in the way public water systems are. You are responsible for testing.
