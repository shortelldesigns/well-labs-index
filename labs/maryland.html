Maryland labs

Two paths are independently verified for private well owners through official Maryland agencies: (1) your local health department submitting samples to the Maryland Department of Health (MDH) Laboratories Administration — Water Microbiology (private well sample reports are mailed to the submitting local health department; Well Water Testing Fees are published on MDH’s site); (2) MDE Be Well Wise guidance to test periodically at a state-certified drinking-water laboratory. The table below lists 22 in-state commercial laboratories from MDE’s Maryland’s Certified Drinking Water Laboratories PDF (Latest Revisions: 10/1/2026, retrieved 13 October 2026). Municipal/utility plant labs and out-of-state labs are omitted. About 350,000 Maryland households rely on private wells; private wells are not regulated like public systems. Call before you collect water. Test first.

Verified for private well owners

Local health department → MDH Laboratories Administration (Water Microbiology)

MDH Water Microbiology · Well Water Testing Fees · MDE Be Well Wise (local health departments)

The MDH Water Microbiology Laboratory analyzes potable and other water samples. Its public page states that private well sample reports are mailed to the submitting local health department. MDH also publishes a Well Water Testing Fees schedule (Bacteriology, Nitrate-Nitrate, Turbidity, Copper, Iron, Lead, Hardness, and other listed analytes). Start with your county health department to arrange bottles, drop-off, and submission. Laboratory phone as printed on the Water Microbiology page: 443-681-3960 · Supervisor Erinna Kinney 443-681-3948.

Fees: Confirm current amounts on the MDH Well Water Testing Fees page — this directory does not republish fee lines in the table.

MDE Be Well Wise — state-certified laboratory path

Be Well Wise · Maryland’s Certified Drinking Water Laboratories (PDF) · Laboratory Certification Program · UMD Extension Water Testing

MDE states about 350,000 Maryland households rely on private wells and that owners are responsible for ensuring drinking-water safety. Have water tested periodically by a state-certified laboratory. At a minimum, sample annually for total coliform bacteria, nitrates, total dissolved solids, and pH; also sample after changes in odor, taste, or appearance. Local health departments, University of Maryland Extension, and others can help identify local contaminant concerns. Water Well Construction Program: 410-537-3784. For private residential / real estate / non-compliance testing, MDE’s LCP page recommends using a certified sampler. Call each lab on the certified list to confirm they will take your private-well sample, which analytes they offer, shipping instructions, and current fees.

Methodology and disclosure · State directory

This site is not a government agency and does not certify laboratories. Listings are transcribed from official state and federal sources cited on each page. Accreditation, phone numbers, and sample-acceptance policies change. Confirm with the laboratory and the state certification program before you ship a sample.

Private wells are not regulated under the federal Safe Drinking Water Act in the way public water systems are. You are responsible for testing.
