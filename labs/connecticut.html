Connecticut labs

The Connecticut Department of Public Health (DPH) Environmental Laboratory Certification Program (ELCP) publishes a list of in-state approved commercial laboratories certified to test drinking water. The list notes that all of these labs are approved to test drinking water, but only some offer testing for private wells. The table below transcribes all 17 labs from the list dated 10/09/2026; 13 are marked “Private Well Testing.” Call before you collect water. Test first.

Testing a private well in Connecticut: confirming a lab

CT DPH’s Environmental Laboratory Certification Program (ELCP) registers and approves environmental laboratories in Connecticut and certifies them for testing drinking water and other samples for microbiologicals, inorganics, organics and radiochemicals. It also enforces EPA regulations on environmental laboratory testing under the Safe Drinking Water Act. ELCP publishes separate lists of in-state labs certified to test drinking water, out-of-state approved commercial labs, lead-testing labs, and labs certified for PFOS and PFOA. To confirm a lab, find it on the current ELCP drinking-water list for the test you need. The ELCP page we read does not list recommended private-well tests, so we do not state any here.

Source: CT DPH Environmental Laboratory Certification, read 26 October 2026.

Methodology and disclosure · State directory

This site is not a government agency and does not certify laboratories. Listings are transcribed from official state and federal sources cited on each page. Accreditation, phone numbers, and sample-acceptance policies change. Confirm with the laboratory and the state certification program before you ship a sample.

Private wells are not regulated under the federal Safe Drinking Water Act in the way public water systems are. You are responsible for testing.
