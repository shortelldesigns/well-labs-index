North Carolina labs

One path is independently verified for private-well chemical testing: submit samples to the North Carolina State Laboratory of Public Health through your local health department. The table below lists 47 in-state laboratories from NCSLPH’s Certified Drinking Water Commercial Laboratories roster (retrieved 6 September 2026). That roster does not say which commercial labs accept homeowner / private-well samples. Call before you collect water.

  • Charlotte Water – Environmental Lab Services appears on the commercial certified list and is kept as printed.
  • Prices are intentionally omitted. Certification summaries may truncate long analyte lists with “….”

Verified for private well owners

North Carolina State Laboratory of Public Health (via local health department)

Contact your county Environmental Health program first · Find your local health department · State Lab hub: slph.dph.ncdhhs.gov

NCSLPH’s Environmental Inorganic Chemistry page (retrieved 6 September 2026) states a private water system (homeowner) can obtain chemical analyses from this Laboratory only if the sample is submitted through the local health department. NC DHHS Private Well Water Testing FAQs likewise say State Lab testing is scheduled through the local health department, and that existing well owners can also contact a state-certified commercial laboratory. Collection kits for health departments, holding times, and fees are not copied here — call your local health department before you collect. Prices are intentionally omitted.

Source: Environmental Inorganic Chemistry · Private Well Water Testing FAQs · Private Wells program.

Methodology and disclosure · State directory

This site is not a government agency and does not certify laboratories. Listings are transcribed from official state and federal sources cited on each page. Accreditation, phone numbers, and sample-acceptance policies change. Confirm with the laboratory and the state certification program before you ship a sample.

Private wells are not regulated under the federal Safe Drinking Water Act in the way public water systems are. You are responsible for testing.
