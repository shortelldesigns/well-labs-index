Alabama labs

Two paths are independently verified for private wells: (1) ADPH county health departments and the Bureau of Clinical Laboratories (BCL) for bacteriological testing (total coliform / E. coli) — kits from your county health department or BCL Prattville; (2) ADEM-certified drinking water laboratories for chemical / nitrate / metals testing when a lab will take a private-well sample — ADEM explicitly says consumers may use certified labs to test private wells, but commercial acceptance is unknown unless you verify with the lab. The table below lists 10 in-state commercial / independent laboratories from ADEM’s Certified Bacteriological Laboratories (Revised October 30, 2023) and In-State Certified Chemical Laboratories (Revised November 6, 2023) PDFs. Municipal and water-system plant labs are omitted. Call before you collect water.

Verified for private well owners

ADPH county health department / Bureau of Clinical Laboratories — bacterial testing

ADPH Well Water · Private water collection instructions (PDF) · Bureau of Clinical Laboratories

ADPH says about 800,000 Alabamians (about 20% of the state) depend on private wells, and that if you get drinking water from your own well, you are responsible for ensuring it is safe. For bacteriological testing you may call your county health department and request that an environmentalist collect the sample (there may be a collection fee), or collect the specimen yourself using sterilized bottles from your local county health department or BCL.

Prattville Laboratory: 204 Legends Court, Prattville, AL 36066 (courier / UPS / FedEx); P.O. Box 1000, Prattville, AL 36067 (USPS). Phones on the instructions form: (334) 290-3201 · form header (334) 290-6130. Bacteriological PDF also lists Prattville Branch Laboratory contact (334) 260-3400.

Mobile Division Laboratory: 757 Museum Drive, Mobile, AL 36608 · (251) 344-6049.

Samples are tested for total coliform bacteria (including E. coli). Deliver during normal work hours Monday through Thursday, 7:30 am – 4:00 pm. Samples will not be tested if over 30 hours old when received. Delivery is the individual’s responsibility. Guidance: Bureau of Environmental Services staff at a local county health department or the RSA Tower (Montgomery) (334) 206-5373.

Important: The Bureau of Clinical Laboratories does not perform any chemical analysis of private well water. The water test report is designed to meet ADEM and EPA specifications; ADPH notes it does not meet regulatory or financial-institution requirements such as mortgage. The instructions PDF states the fee is currently waived for each private water sample — confirm current policy with ADPH or your county. This directory does not publish a statewide price.

County finder: ADPH’s linked locations / county health department and health-departments URLs soft-404’d at retrieve time. Use the working ACES county health department list instead, or search your county’s ADPH office.

Chemical / nitrate / metals — ADEM-certified labs (call first)

ACES advises that for more comprehensive tests (e.g., nitrates or alkalinity) you may need to reach out to a certified drinking water lab and ask what they offer private well owners. ADEM’s laboratory certification program page states: “Consumers may also consider the use of a certified laboratory to test private wells.” Contact: Drinking Water Branch (334) 271-7773.

Methodology and disclosure · State directory

This site is not a government agency and does not certify laboratories. Listings are transcribed from official state and federal sources cited on each page. Accreditation, phone numbers, and sample-acceptance policies change. Confirm with the laboratory and the state certification program before you ship a sample.

Private wells are not regulated under the federal Safe Drinking Water Act in the way public water systems are. You are responsible for testing.
