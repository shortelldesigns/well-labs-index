Missouri labs

One path is independently verified for private wells: Missouri State Public Health Laboratory (SPHL) analyzes private drinking-water samples for bacteria and chemical panels when you get a collection kit through your local public health agency (or State District Health Office). DHSS states it does not offer testing for real estate transactions. The tables below list in-state laboratories marked Commercial=Yes on Missouri DNR’s certified chemical and microbiological Excel downloads (retrieved 8 September 2026). DNR does not say which commercial labs accept homeowner samples — call before you collect water.

Sources retrieved 8 September 2026. Private-well kit path: DHSS Well Water Test Kit Information and Services · SPHL bacteriological testing · SPHL chemical testing · Find your LPHA. Certified lab hub: Missouri DNR Certified Laboratories · Chemical · Microbiological.

Verified for private well owners

Missouri State Public Health Laboratory (via local public health agency)

101 N. Chestnut Street / PO Box 570, Jefferson City, MO 65102-0570 · (573) 751-3334 · Start with your local public health agency for a kit · Optional online form linked from DHSS: Request a Kit

DHSS Well Water Test Kit Information and Services (retrieved 8 September 2026) states the Missouri State Public Health Laboratory offers both bacteria and chemical private drinking water testing. SPHL’s bacteriological page states property owners may submit private-well samples in MSPHL collection kits available through local public health agencies or State District Health Offices, and that a $10 handling fee (check or money order only) must be included with the bacterial sample. Chemical testing pages describe New Well Series and Full Panel options and likewise point well owners to the LPHA for kits. DHSS offers private well testing as a public health service but does not offer testing for real estate transactions. Confirm kit instructions, holding times (bacterial samples must reach the lab and testing must start within 48 hours of collection per the SPHL bacteriological page), courier options, and current fees with your LPHA before you collect.

Source: Well Water Test Kit Information and Services · SPHL bacteriological testing · SPHL chemical testing · LPHA directory.

Methodology and disclosure · State directory

This site is not a government agency and does not certify laboratories. Listings are transcribed from official state and federal sources cited on each page. Accreditation, phone numbers, and sample-acceptance policies change. Confirm with the laboratory and the state certification program before you ship a sample.

Private wells are not regulated under the federal Safe Drinking Water Act in the way public water systems are. You are responsible for testing.
