Ohio labs

One laboratory is independently verified to take private-well samples: Mahoning County Board of Health (listed that way on Ohio EPA’s list; the county health department’s own page says it tests private drinking-water systems). The other rows are Ohio commercial, independent, or local-health-department laboratories transcribed from Ohio EPA’s Combined Lab List dated August 2026, rev. 8/13/2026. We have not verified that those labs currently accept private-well samples. Call before you collect water.

Sources retrieved 29 August 2026. Official list: Combined-Lab-List.pdf (Ohio EPA Division of Drinking and Ground Waters). Ohio EPA private-well FAQ (updated 2 September 2025): Testing water from private wells.

What we could and could not verify

  • Ohio EPA does not regulate or test private wells. It certifies laboratories for public drinking water and points homeowners to this list, then to the local health department. Source: Ohio EPA FAQ, updated 2 September 2025.
  • The Combined Lab List is titled for analyses on public drinking water. Appearance on it is not an endorsement and is not a statement that the lab takes homeowner samples. Ohio EPA: other laboratories may hold acceptance and may not appear (no commercial services, pending applications). Questions: dwlabcert@epa.ohio.gov or 614-644-2752.
  • Municipal water-treatment-plant, water-department, water-works, water-resources, sanitary-engineering, and water-company laboratories were omitted. The list does not say those plants take homeowner samples.
  • Out-of-state laboratories on the same PDF (mostly PFAS, radiochemistry, Cryptosporidium, asbestos) were not tabulated. They remain on the official file.
  • Nitrate is marked Yes when the PDF lists Standard Chemistry (the legend includes Nitrate) or lists Nitrate/Nitrite by name. Trace metals are marked Yes when the PDF lists Trace Metals (legend includes arsenic, lead, manganese, and others). The PDF says to contact the lab for the analyses offered.

Start with your local health department

Ohio EPA: before calling a certified laboratory, contact your local health department. Local health districts regulate private water wells and may have a homeowner testing program. Find yours: Ohio Department of Health, find local health districts.

Ohio Department of Health Private Water Systems Program: 246 N. High Street, Columbus, Ohio 43215 · 614-644-7558 · Privatewater@odh.ohio.gov. Program page: odh.ohio.gov/…/private-water-systems-program.

Methodology and disclosure · State directory

This site is not a government agency and does not certify laboratories. Listings are transcribed from official state and federal sources cited on each page. Accreditation, phone numbers, and sample-acceptance policies change. Confirm with the laboratory and the state certification program before you ship a sample.

Private wells are not regulated under the federal Safe Drinking Water Act in the way public water systems are. You are responsible for testing.
