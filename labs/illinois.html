Illinois labs

One path is independently verified for private wells: most Illinois local health departments can test for coliform bacteria and nitrate yearly, per the Illinois EPA Well Water Testing page. IEPA does not name individual health departments that accept drop-off — call your county first. The tables below list in-state IEPA primary chemical labs that look like commercial/for-hire analytical laboratories (document date 12/09/2025 in the official PDF filename) and in-state IDPH certified water microbiology labs marked Commercial or Health Department (Revision Date Thursday, December 4, 2025). Neither roster labels private-well acceptance for commercial rows — call before you collect water. Illinois State Water Survey’s HEAL no longer provides testing for individual households.

Sources retrieved 10 September 2026. Private-well guidance: IEPA Well Water Testing · LHD finder: alphabetical Local Health Departments · IDPH health regions map. Certified lab hub: IEPA Accredited Labs. HEAL note: ISWS HEAL.

Verified for private well owners

Local health departments — coliform bacteria and nitrate

Find your department: Illinois Local Health Departments search · IDPH health regions map

IEPA’s Well Water Testing page for private well users (retrieved 10 September 2026) states that coliform bacteria and nitrates should be tested on a yearly basis and can be done by most local health departments. The same page states certified labs also test well water and may be your only option for certain chemicals, and that you should consult your local health department about other contaminants and testing frequency. This is not a single state public health laboratory mail-in path. Call your LHD before you collect a sample — acceptance, kits, holding times, and fees are not named individually on the IEPA page. Prices intentionally omitted.

Source: IEPA Well Water Testing · LHD alphabetical listing · LHD regional map.

ISWS HEAL — not available for households

The Illinois State Water Survey Health & Environmental Applications Laboratory (HEAL) page states: “HEAL no longer provides testing services for individual households.” Do not treat ISWS/HEAL as a verified private-well lab. Source: ISWS HEAL.

Methodology and disclosure · State directory

This site is not a government agency and does not certify laboratories. Listings are transcribed from official state and federal sources cited on each page. Accreditation, phone numbers, and sample-acceptance policies change. Confirm with the laboratory and the state certification program before you ship a sample.

Private wells are not regulated under the federal Safe Drinking Water Act in the way public water systems are. You are responsible for testing.
